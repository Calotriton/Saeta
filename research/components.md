# Arrow components: research summary

Companion to `components.json`. Values marked **(approx)** are typical ranges taken from common practice or inferred from wood density. Values marked **(uncertain)** could not be checked against a manufacturer source. Manufacturer tables were verified on 2026-11-22.

## Units and spine systems
- 1 grain = 0.0648 g. GPI = grains per inch of bare shaft.
- **Wood spine (lbs):** AMO/ATA standard, 2 lb load on a 26" span. Shafts are sold in 5-lb groups (40-45#, 45-50#…). For example, 45-50# ≈ 0.575-0.520" deflection.
- **Carbon/aluminium spine:** deflection in thousandths of an inch, 1.94 lb load on a 28" span (500 = 0.500"). A lower number means a stiffer shaft. Wood and carbon spine numbers cannot be converted one-to-one, so use each maker's chart.

## A. Shafts

### Wood
- Diameters: 9/32 (7.1 mm), 5/16 (7.9), 21/64 (8.3), 11/32 (8.7), 23/64 (9.1), 3/8 (9.5).
- Typical spine ranges by diameter (they vary by supplier):
  - 5/16: about 20-45#
  - 11/32: about 35/40-65/70#
  - 23/64: about 50-85#
- Suppliers' spine-group listings: 3Rivers POC 11/32 comes in 40-45 to 70-75#, weight-matched in 20-grain groups; 23/64 runs 50-55 to 75-80#. Other charts list 5/16 at 30-40#.
- Weight scales with diameter². Relative to 11/32: 5/16 ≈ ×0.83, 23/64 ≈ ×1.09, 3/8 ≈ ×1.19.
- GPI by species at 11/32 **(approx, from density)**. One forum estimate gives POC ≈ 12.2 gpi, Sitka ≈ 11.1 and Douglas fir ≈ 13.5.

  | Species | gpi @11/32 | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | Port Orford cedar | 10-13.5 | Reference arrow wood; expensive |
  | Sitka / European spruce | 9.5-12 | Light and strong |
  | Nordic pine | 11-14.5 | Common EU supplier wood (Superschaft, Bearpaw); stiffer |
  | Douglas fir | 12-14.5 | |
  | Larch | 12.5-15 | (uncertain) |
  | Poplar | 10-12.5 | Soft; most Mary Rose arrows were poplar |
  | Birch | 14-17 | |
  | Ash | 15-18 | |
  | Beech | 16-18.5 | |
  | Hickory | 18-21 | Heaviest; used for footings |

- Shaft profiles: parallel, tapered (11/32 → 5/16 at the nock), barrelled, breasted.
- Bamboo: Tonkin cane is heavy, stiff and durable. One forum report gives 390-510 gr for 33" shafts **(approx)**. Hollow ends need a hardwood plug or a cut near a node. Arundo or river cane usually needs a hardwood foreshaft.

### Fiberglass
- Tubes are usually 5/16" OD. Solid rods are about 5-7 mm.
- These arrows are heavy and cheap, for bows up to about 30-35 lb **(approx)**.
- Broken fiberglass leaves glass splinters, so throw damaged arrows away.

## B. Points
- **Glue-on points for wood or bamboo** sit on a 5° taper. The nock taper is 11°. Diameters are 5/16, 11/32 and 23/64.
  - Typical weights: 70-125 gr (5/16) and 100-225 gr (11/32, 23/64). 3Rivers sells 200/225 gr glue-on points.
- **Screw-in points** use an 8-32 thread. They go into a carbon or aluminium insert, or into a glued-on wood taper adapter. Weights run from 75 to 300 gr.
- **One-piece glue-in points** are sized by carbon ID or aluminium size (bullet/NIBB).
- **Other point types:** steel blunt, rubber blunt (slips on), judo, broadhead, forged bodkin and medieval heads (socketed), stone or bone, LARP foam heads (organiser-certified only) and whistling points.
- **Legality:** bowhunting is banned in many European countries, for example Germany, the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium and Ireland **(verify)**. It is regulated in Spain, France, Portugal and Denmark. In Spain each autonomous community regulates hunting (Constitución art. 148.1.11; Ley 1/1970 applies where a region has no law of its own).

## D. Fletching
- **Feather weights:** a Trueflight 5" shield weighs 3.2 gr; a 4" round back weighs 2.3 gr (Lancaster/Abbey listings). Approximate weights for other sizes are in the JSON.
- **Wing:** never mix left- and right-wing feathers on one arrow. A helical clamp must be the same hand as the wing. Either wing works for right- or left-handed archers.
- **Shapes:** shield, parabolic, banana, low-profile, triangle (P&Y), round back, long historical, flu-flu and spiral flu-flu.
- **Lengths:** 2-6".
- **Configurations:** 3 × 120°, 4 × 90°, 4 × 75/105° and 6 for a flu-flu. Orientation can be straight, offset or helical.
- **Feathers vs vanes:** shooting off the shelf or the hand requires feathers. Vanes need an elevated rest.

## E. Extras
- Finishing: sealer/lacquer, crown dip (7-10"), cresting, wraps (4" or 7", sized by OD; for carbon or aluminium), thread binding.
- Strength and weight: footing (wood), brass inserts, half-outs and outserts, weight tubes, screw-in point weights (Black Eagle 5/10/30 gr), impact collars.
- Identification: personal marking.

## F. Rules
There are 38 rules in `components.json → rules`. Each has an `id`, a `level` (require / forbid / recommend / warn), machine-style `if`/`then` text, and `es`/`en` sentences. They cover:
- ID matching and nock/point systems for each shaft material
- feathers only when shooting off the shelf or hand; vanes only with a rest
- horse-bow and thumb-draw rules
- wing consistency and cock-feather position
- spine heuristics, FOC and minimum arrow weight
- broadhead/bodkin/LARP safety and legality
- competition classes that require wood **(uncertain, federation-dependent)**
